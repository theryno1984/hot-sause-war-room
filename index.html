Skill first, quarterback second

Taking the best runner or receiver at #31 and the quarterback at #43 beats the reverse by roughly 14 points. The skill board falls from +34 to +17 to +6 across your first three picks. Quarterback decay between #31 and #43 is zero.

Goff survives to #43 in 84% of simulations. At #55 a tier-2 quarterback is there only 2% of the time — that is a cliff, not a slope.

The running back pool is gone before you pick

Seven of the eight teams ahead of you need a back, and two of them kept none at all. The model has roughly thirteen backs off the board in the eighteen picks before your first.

That is why McCaffrey and Walker are worth more than their raw numbers suggest — the realistic alternative at your pick is a +40 back, not a +90 one.

Path probability = NFL draft capital × depth-chart position × incumbent fragility (age, injury, projection gap). Ceiling = the blocker’s role scaled by draft capital — a first-rounder inherits the job, a seventh-rounder inherits a committee slice. Ceiling is additionally discounted by depth — a third-stringer must climb past two bodies, not just one. These weights are reasoned estimates, not fitted to historical rookie outcomes.
